# Common 300-car matrix

The selection preserves all 57 accepted editions, their prepared geometry, and their individual physics profiles. The only preceding profile corrections are documented in `../fleet57-next-calibration/`. An additional 150 GT4 and 93 GT1 editions bring the roster to 300. Editions represent source models, not 300 unique real-world nameplates. Selection cycles through source name groups and excludes only static data/geometry incompatibilities before physics testing. The native catalog, full decoded stock rows, every exclusion and asset preparation receipts are retained. The initial unqualified selection is retained under `attempt0-unqualified-native/`; no physical results from it were used for selection.

The new editions use explicit game-derived proxies. GT4 displayed metric PS converts by 0.73549875 kW/PS, torque by raw/100 × 9.80665 Nm, RPM bytes by 100, simple stock ratios and final drive by 1000. Gear-flagged and extra-final-drive configurations are excluded until their native consumers are resolved. Native display statistics do not recreate the source game's complete engine modifier chain. Missing displayed peak RPMs remain estimates. GT1 stock HP and kgm values use their documented conversions; gearing is estimated. Drive layouts are decoded, but AWD split is estimated. Tires, CG, suspension, drag, delivery and shifts remain declared priors rather than real-world measurements. `prepared-physics.json` records evidence for every field.

Every car receives the exact same 24 original city scenarios and 5,000 new proving-ground definitions. There are two executions of each pair in fresh worlds: 300 × 5,024 × 2 = 3,014,400 executions. The new definitions are ten 500-case grids: launch, brake, coast, steering step, slalom, trail brake, handbrake, reverse, slope and bumps. The grids vary speed, input, duration, frequency, heading and track geometry. Heading variations explicitly check rotational consistency. No car-specific input, threshold or skipped case is permitted. Scenario and source manifests are frozen before the matrix starts.

The proving ground adds an 8 km square support pad, slopes from −5° to +5°, and five transverse 1–5 cm bumps of varied width. Original city geometry and route definitions are preserved. The new fixtures run the actual production VehiclePlugin, fixed at 120 Hz, with 360 settling updates and fresh physics worlds for both repeats. Original cases use the production ScenarioPlugin, CityPlugin, AssemblyPlugin, command bus and route follower. A small optional in-memory ScenarioOutcome allows compact execution without recording millions of bundles. A regression compares full production application and batch state at every tick, including launch, steering/braking and reset, on the 350Z and all four protected cars.

Each pair retains both full-state SHA-256 hashes and measured metrics. The 41 float fields per tick cover transform, linear/angular velocity, RPM, gear, steering, speed, slip, reset count, suspension travel, wheel spin, drive demand, longitudinal wheel forces, effective input and contact count. Full binary traces are retained for failures and repeat differences. Identical repeat hashes share one retained failure trace; otherwise both traces remain. Lossless gzip compression verifies uncompressed trace hashes before removing the raw duplicate. Successful cases retain metrics and trace digests rather than all ticks. This is a compact numerical evaluation, distinct from a portable recorder/replay bundle. The separate native acceptance run retains renderer evidence for every car.

Numerical invariants flag nonfinite state, unexpected reset, rollover above 80°, speed above 150 m/s, absence of all wheel support, missing propulsion response, coasting energy increase, and missing braking response. They are stability/response guards, not real-world calibration targets. Stops not reached inside a scenario horizon are explicitly censored. Continued brake input can engage reverse after stopping under the existing control convention, so first-stop distance and minimum speed are kept separately from final speed. All original scenario assertions and route outcomes remain authoritative for those cases. Failures are retained and evaluated, not excluded from the fleet or converted to passes.

The 57-car recorder matrix uses its separately frozen pre-batch executable and clean asset set. Expanded assets were initially staged in the active asset directory, causing explicit provenance omissions in 143 attempts; those attempts were retained and excluded from the canonical complete-evidence matrix. The correction reruns only incomplete/interrupted tasks with identical profiles, binary and scenarios. A retry coordinator's relative-path indexing error was corrected; completed evidence was recovered with an explicit receipt and unavailable elapsed times stay null. These evidence-preparation incidents do not change vehicle physics or suppress behavioral failures.

A throughput-only attempt using default task pools was interrupted and retained under `artifacts/fleet300-attempt0-default-pools-20261025/`. The final compact fixtures use one task-pool thread and disable asynchronous static transform dirty-tree tracking. Differential trials preserve all 1,000 proving-ground pairs and all 24 city pairs exactly, including the per-tick 41-field hashes and metrics. Under concurrent load the compact trial takes 73.2 s versus 183.3 s for the intermediate pool-only trial. The original lightly loaded 69.6 s trial is not a same-load speedup baseline. `compact-comparison.json`, `serial-comparison.json` and `throughput-attempt-disposition.json` retain timings, binaries and dispositions. The final matrix starts fresh for all cars/cases. The coordinator source at dispatch is retained separately; later progress-flush tooling changes do not alter the running process or physics.

The first complete sweep exposes downhill resets when the supported vehicle reaches the existing city safety boundary at world Y < −12 m. The Viper case 4409 trace reaches Y = −12.012 m while still following the −5° surface. This is an incompatible track fixture, not a justification to change vehicle power or the game’s fall protection. All 500 slope definitions are revised to a common 128 m base height. A regression reproduces the reset on the original ground and verifies the raised course on the Viper, Zonda and four protected cars. The full set of 500 slope cases is rerun twice on every one of the 300 cars; no car-specific exemption is used.

The original 3,014,400 executions are retained as a complete pre-track-fix cohort. The additional 300,000 slope executions and 10,048 repaired-profile executions produce 3,324,448 matrix executions in total. Final common coverage remains 5,024 cases × 300 cars × 2: 4,500 unchanged proving-ground cases, 500 revised slope cases, and 24 unchanged city cases per car. Source comparison proves the only revised files are the case definition and proving-ground fixture; all production library sources, profiles and geometry remain exact. Eight representative non-slope trace pairs also remain exact. Original and revised failure counts are reported separately. High-speed rollover flags are retained as physical stress findings, with CG/tire/inertia uncertainty explicit, rather than tuning unsupported parameters merely to clear the guard.


The baseline driving contract revealed one native-data import defect: GT1 ConceptCar (`exp-161`) has positive rated power but zero torque and torque-RPM placeholders. Raw native fields remain unchanged in the source dataset. The importer now treats those zeros as unavailable and uses a clearly marked minimum-consistent torque estimate from power × 9549.297 / power RPM, placing the estimated torque peak at the power peak. Only this profile changes; the other 299 profiles and all geometry remain exact. The missing native torque curve is still unresolved. All 5,024 cases are rerun twice for this car with identical inputs, and its revised slope traces must agree exactly with the common all-car slope rerun.

The old six-second 15 m/s baseline acceleration requirement was a sports-car assumption that rejects the 18 PS Fiat 500F. The shared basic-mobility contract now requires 5 m/s, without changing any original scenario assertion or profile-specific acceleration regression. The Fiat's original reverse-recovery displacement failure remains a reported same-scenario result. The only production source-file difference for this contract is inside the test module; executable force logic stays exact. Original case-summary display prefixes differ (`handling-` versus `fleet40-`); the bridge checks complete execution definitions after vehicle binding, plus all assertion/readiness/tick outcomes and final states, while excluding only labels, recorder artifact lists and elapsed wall time.


The final native recorder caches the roster asset allowlist once per capture instead of reparsing it for every file. The exported membership policy is unchanged and has a negative control for unlisted GLBs. Four shutdown integration controls serialize their simultaneous durable asset captures while retaining their original 8/10-second deadlines. Numeric workers were briefly suspended during these startup controls and resumed before completion; the pause receipt records exact process IDs and times. The completed common matrix is unaffected. The opt-in recorder export ceiling increases from 2 to 8 GiB for 300-car capture volume, with an upper-bound regression and the normal 512 MiB default unchanged. These provenance/configuration changes differ from the frozen numerical binary; vehicle force, scenario, city and geometry code remain exact. The first native dispatch rejected the unsupported 8 GiB option before any vehicle ran; its failed log and binary are retained as attempt 2.


A native scalability attempt stopped after 64 published passing cars when the aggregate `report.json` crossed the ordinary 2 MiB metadata ceiling. No vehicle acceptance failure was reported. The interrupted bundle, binary and reviews remain separate as attempt 5. A shared policy now grants only the root aggregate report a 16 MiB bound in both publication and provenance capture; ordinary metadata and nested per-car reports retain 2 MiB. An end-to-end regression publishes and exports 300 report rows with 2,640 frame-time samples each. The final native cohort starts all 300 cars from one corrected binary, rather than combining the incomplete capture attempt with later results. This affects diagnostic export only; the completed numerical cohorts are unchanged.


The next native attempt passed 93 cars before MINI ONE entered braking below the original 5 m/s prerequisite after hitting a city-block edge during its steering maneuver. The trace rises from about 0.50 m to 1.64 m as speed falls from about 18 m/s to 4.7 m/s. The all-car regression requires entry speed above the original 5 m/s threshold and height rise below 0.4 m; all pass. Interactive play and all 24 recorded/numerical city scenarios retain their original geometry. `native-corridor-validation.json` binds the test and source. The final root-report bound is 32 MiB for complete 300-car frame-time data; an end-to-end control exports 300 rows with 4,800 samples each, while ordinary metadata stays at 2 MiB. The interrupted 94-row native cohort and its reviews are retained separately as attempt 6. The final native run again starts all 300 cars with one binary and the common clear corridor.
