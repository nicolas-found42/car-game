# Remaining reference uncertainties

Research date: 2026-10-25. Website text was read through Jev browser; search was used for discovery. The companion [remaining-targets.json](remaining-targets.json) distinguishes measured observations, claims and diagnostic comparisons. No production parameters were edited by this research.

The next supported candidates are the 350Z's excessive effective midrange delivery and the early NSX's optimistic lateral grip. The Supra's apparent launch deficit disappears against a more closely related 1997 test. The Viper's matching 100 mph result argues against raising power to fit its launch alone. GNX prototype results, the 911's load basis and CSL track peaks still cannot establish exact calibration targets.

## Nissan 350Z

The original August 2002 C/D test is a **2003 US Track six-speed**, 3322 lb, 287 hp at 6200 rpm and 274 lb-ft at 4800 rpm. It uses Bridgestone RE040 225/45WR18 front and 245/45WR18 rear; 0–60 is 5.4 s, 0–100 mph 13.9 s, 70–0 is 164 ft and grip is **0.88 g on a 300 ft circle**. Thus the elapsed 60–100 interval is 8.5 s, without needing to assume a rollout correction. Its light wheels, Brembo brakes and lift-reducing spoilers distinguish it from the unspecified GT4 equipment grade. The current 1506.853 kg profile matches this car's 3322 lb, so adding mass to absorb the discrepancy is unsupported. [Original C/D test](https://www.caranddriver.com/reviews/a15136959/nissan-350z-road-test/).

Autoweek independently tested a **2003 Track six-speed** on the same tire sizes at California Speedway: 5.66 s to 60 mph, 14.32 s at 99.7 mph through the quarter, 114 ft from 60 mph and **0.86 g on a 200 ft circle**. It reports grinding understeer and reaching 60 at the top of second, just below 6600 rpm. Its stated 3225 lb differs from C/D by 97 lb; do not replace C/D's measured-car basis or average the two. Its 6.26 s to 100 km/h is retained as printed, despite the unusually large 0.60 s step from 60 mph. [Original Autoweek test](https://www.autoweek.com/news/a2115721/2003-nissan-350z-its-z-lightful-z-back-long-live-z/).

The original R&T specification scan indexed at the linked attachment gives 5.6/14.2 s to 60/100 mph, 14.3 s at 100.2 mph through the quarter and 0.89 g. Browser retrieval returned 403, so these are **indexed original-scan corroboration**, not independently inspected full-page evidence; its circle diameter and payload are not verified. [R&T original scan, third-party host](https://www.rx8club.com/attachments/rx-8-racing-25/stats-rx-8-34712/2003_nissan350z-24354d1090995725).

The OEM FS6R31A table gives 3.794/2.324/1.624/1.271/1.000/0.794, matching the simulation. The FIA-affiliated Australian recognition sheet separately records these gears and a 3.538 rear viscous LSD final drive. The manual page was indexed but its browser reader hit a security check; the recognition PDF started a download rather than rendering in Jev. These corroborate existing gearing, not a proposed ratio change. [Nissan manual MT-18 reproduction](https://ownersmanuals2.com/nissan/350z-2003-repair-manual-manual-transmission-section-mt-46186/page-18), [Motorsport Australia Track recognition](https://motorsport.org.au/wpblob0fe832abcb/wp-content/uploads/docs/default-source/sporting-technical/group-3e/3-09-031-nissan-350z-track.pdf?x58319=).

**Decision:** a delivery-only probe can be supported while retaining nominal output, mass, gearing, launch traction and current qualified braking/grip. Compare the whole acceleration curve against C/D, with R&T as qualified corroboration. The 13.9–14.2 s observations are a related-equipment diagnostic range, not a universal factory acceptance interval. No retrieved source establishes a measured drivetrain-loss percentage, test payload, or rollout.

## Early Acura NSX

C/D's 2019 retrospective explicitly assigns the September 1990 results to the **1991 NSX**, 270 hp, five-speed manual, 3020 lb, and Yokohama A-022 205/50R15 + 225/50R16. It reproduces 5.2/13.3 s to 60/100 mph, 157 ft from 70 mph and **0.86 g on a 300 ft circle**. The 2019 borrowed museum car wears modern RE71R tires; those are **not** the equipment behind the old numbers. This resolves the original digital article's ambiguous 1990 versus 1991 label and original tire identity. It does **not** establish a VIN, build date or production/preproduction status for the instrumented car. [Publisher retrospective and explicitly dated table](https://www.caranddriver.com/reviews/comparison-test/a28930414/2019-honda-civic-type-r-vs-1991-acura-nsx/).

The original comparison warns that ambient temperature sometimes exceeded 100°F and weather corrections might incompletely compensate; it specifically connects this warning to power-related results. It does not quantify a grip correction. Keep the caveat; do not invent a weather-normalized skidpad number. [Original September 1990 comparison](https://www.caranddriver.com/reviews/comparison-test/a15140663/porsche-911-carrera-4-cabriolet-vs-acura-nsx-chevrolet-corvette-zr-1-ferrari-348ts-lotus-esprit-turbo-se-archived-comparison-test/).

Independent original R&T August 1990 text, reproduced by an owner archive, describes a red/black five-speed on A-022 205/50ZR15 + 225/50ZR16 and 15/16-inch forged wheels. It differentiates the production package from the shorter 1989 first-stage prototype. It reports 5.7 s to 60, 14.0 s through the quarter and **0.93 g on a makeshift skidpad with bumps and pavement seams**, promising a normal-site retest. Circle diameter, timing rollout and payload are absent; a VIN or final production-build status is not proven by the prose. The full original magazine scan was not retrieved, so this is an original-article transcription with custody limitations. [R&T August 1990 transcription](https://rednsx.org/articles/rt90.html).

Acura's June 3, 1990 factory specifications index confirms the 1991 manual's 270 hp/7100 rpm, 210 lb-ft/5300 rpm, 8000 rpm redline and original A-022 sizes. Direct browser access to both Acura and Honda hosts returned 403; retain the indexed-only retrieval status. [Factory specifications](https://acuranews.com/en-US/releases/release-ff6633ebcc698302413a3d004c34ca74-1991-acura-nsx-specifications).

**Decision:** 0.86–0.93 g is a useful **period, equipment-matched observation range with unmatched test surfaces/circle**, not a precise 300 ft acceptance band. Current 0.986 g exceeds even its upper observation by about 6%. A conservative lateral-only estimate near the upper range is supportable if explicitly qualified; do not force the hot C/D observation exactly, alter longitudinal tire grip, or claim resolved production-car validation. The later 1994 wheel/tire package and 1997 3.2-litre NSX-T remain excluded.

## Buick GNX

The C/D May 1987 4.7 s result used an early prototype; interviews with its creators explicitly identify the January Arizona event as prototype testing before final approval. The same history says one later production GNX reached Popular Mechanics, but supplies no resulting instrumented table or payload. It cannot convert the earlier result into a production target. [Firsthand creator interviews](https://www.caranddriver.com/features/a23739443/buick-regal-grand-national-gnx-history/).

R&T's original June 1987 article reports Buick's **5.4 s** claim, but its own repeated **5.6–5.7 s** timing is explicitly informal and uses the original well-used prototype at Mesa. Its launch description takes up converter slack against the brake; the article's 300 hp/420 lb-ft prototype engine differs from selected production advertised output. Neither claim nor test supplies controlled payload/rollout. Its printed 15-inch rear-tire size conflicts with the GNX's nominal 16-inch equipment and must not silently become geometry evidence. [Original R&T report](https://www.roadandtrack.com/new-cars/road-tests/a25623/first-look-flashback-1987-buick-gnx/).

No original production-specific curb-mass measurement or GNX factory gearing sheet was verified. The published 1600 kg game proxy remains uncertain; C/D prototype 3545 lb is not a production correction. **Decision:** retire the 4.7 s equality from production mismatch ranking. Retain 5.4 s as a manufacturer-claim diagnostic, not a verified production acceptance envelope; do not raise power to an undocumented “actual” output or invent torque-converter multiplication.

A firsthand 2003 Autoweek feature inspects production GNX 479 and reports factory advertised **276 hp at 4400 rpm / 360 lb-ft at 3000 rpm**, plus 16-inch Gatorbacks and a modified 200-4R/converter. This is better related-production evidence for the torque-peak rpm than the current 2600 rpm prototype proxy, but is still a retrospective report rather than a recovered factory curve. Its quoted 5.5 s/13.43 s at 104 mph has no stated instrumented test identity, payload or protocol; its rounded 3500 lb is not a measured curb-mass correction. [Autoweek's production-car visit](https://www.autoweek.com/news/a2097746/1987-buick-gnx-bad-bone-indeed/).

## Porsche 911 SC 3.0

The existing exact-year dealer-hosted 1982 table pairs 204 PS/150 kW and 267 Nm with 6.8 s to 100 km/h, 1160 kg DIN, 1235 kg EC and 1500 kg maximum. The advertised individual car is a backdate with roll bar and replacement seats, though its engine is described as untuned: the standardized table is not an instrumented test of the listing. No performance payload or launch method is given. The manual page could not be reread because Jev encountered a security check. [Porsche-hosted dealer data](https://finder.porsche.com/ch/de-CH/details/porsche-911-sc-coupe-gebraucht-G028QN?condition=u&model=911).

The newly located original C/D August 1980 test is **US 172 hp**, 2700 lb, P7 205/55VR16 + 225/50VR16, 6.0 s to 60 mph, 18.4 s to 100 mph and 0.80 g on a **218 ft** circle. It is not the 1982 European 204 PS profile and cannot justify changing that car. [Original C/D 1980 test](https://www.caranddriver.com/reviews/a42884417/1980-porsche-911sc-by-the-numbers/).

An indexed secondary pointer identifies ams 26/1982 reporting 5.9 s to 100 km/h at 1180 kg, but the underlying original test was not retrieved; it is not a new target. Porsche's own Cabriolet retrospectives also disagree (6.5 versus 6.8 s) and describe another body. **Decision:** no new mass/delivery calibration is justified. Preserve the earlier load sensitivity as an illustrative probe, without equating half-payload to the separate 6.8 s claim or declaring that claim wrong.

## BMW M3 CSL

BMW's original February 28, 2003 release directly confirms 1385 kg, 265 kW at 7900 rpm, 370 Nm at 4900 rpm, **4.9/16.8 s to 100/200 km/h**, 250 km/h limiter, and a 100–0 claim **under 34 m under optimal conditions**. It identifies Michelin Pilot Sport Cup 235/35ZR19 and 265/30ZR19, special wheels, SMG and launch control. These match the selected configuration; a later BMW retrospective's 4.8/16.7 s remains a separate claim. [Original BMW release](https://www.press.bmwgroup.com/deutschland/article/detail/T0006229DE/bmw-m3-csl-auf-dem-genfer-automobil-salon-2003?language=de).

The primary sport auto retrospective confirms Cup tires and 7:50/1:13.5 track laps with DSC disabled; the original 2003 supertest URL now redirects to 404. Forum reproductions of 1.45 g at the Nordschleife and 1.30 g at Hockenheim are **track peaks**, not 200/300 ft sustained skidpads, and cannot validate a tire coefficient. [Publisher retrospective](https://www.auto-motor-und-sport.de/test/bmw-m3-im-supertest-die-fantastischen-vier-bmw-m3-generationen/), [unavailable original URL](https://www.auto-motor-und-sport.de/supertest/bmw-m3-csl-im-supertest-auf-der-nordschleife-und-dem-hockenheimring-1200550.html).

**Decision:** retain high Cup-tire grip; do not apply ordinary M3 values. Current 33.17 m braking already satisfies the manufacturer's upper bound. Acceleration is somewhat faster than claims, but no matched measured test load has been established for an exact fit.

## Supra, Viper and Elise

**Supra:** original MotorTrend May 1997 comparison identifies a 320 hp six-speed car at **3505 lb on Michelin Pilot SX 235/45ZR17 + 255/40ZR17**, with **5.1/12.3 s** to 60/100 mph and a 13.6 s quarter at 106 mph. Current 5.058 s launch agrees; fitting the older C/D 1993 4.6 s launch would erase real test spread. That earlier 3480 lb RE020-equipped test reports 11.1 s to 100 and 5.9 s rolling 5–60, demonstrating launch/boost sensitivity. Unknown MC3 year/market remains; no power or launch change is supported. [Original 1997 comparison](https://www.motortrend.com/reviews/mitsubishi-3000gt-vr4), [original 1993 C/D test](https://www.caranddriver.com/reviews/a15142755/toyota-supra-turbo-instrumented-test/).

**Viper:** original C/D July 1997 GTS test gives 3410 lb, 450 hp, **4.0/8.8 s** to 60/100, 177 ft braking and **0.95 g/300 ft**. The current 8.775 s to 100 already matches. Article discussion emphasizes launch traction, a broad torque curve, 118 mph quarter trap and no ABS. Rollout/clutch protocol and exact tire size are absent in the accessible comparison. No power increase follows from a launch-only discrepancy. [Original Viper comparison](https://www.caranddriver.com/reviews/a35728264/1997-dodge-viper-gts-vs-porsche-911-turbo-s-vs-acura-nsx-t-comparison-test/).

**Elise 111R:** the January 2004 UK Lotus release reproduction specifies 860 kg in running order, full tank, no occupants/luggage/options; all options raise it to 916 kg. Its 4.9/5.2/13.0 s claims are not assigned a test payload. The existing ratios match this release exactly. Standard UK tires are **RE040 175/55R16 + 225/45R17**; the separately measured US Sport Pack A048 car is not interchangeable. C/D's 12.6 s 2005 first-drive number is explicitly a **manufacturer rating**, and its car is a US prototype around 2000 lb. A brochure search index mislabels power units and valves, so conflicting extracted ratios are not adopted without page inspection. **Decision:** investigate optimistic delivery/grip, but do not treat heavier US equipment or a claim as exact measured validation. [Reproduced UK release](https://elises.co.uk/models/s2_2004/111R/index.html), [C/D first drive and rating label](https://www.caranddriver.com/reviews/a15133679/2005-lotus-elise-road-test-review/), [US Sport Pack instrumented test](https://www.motortrend.com/reviews/0410-sccp-2005-lotus-elise-road-test).
